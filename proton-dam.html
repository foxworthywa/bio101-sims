The proton dam

The electron transport chain doesn't make ATP itself. It uses the energy of falling electrons to pump protons (H⁺) across the inner membrane of the mitochondrion, piling them up like water behind a dam. ATP synthase makes ATP as the protons flow back through it.

The takeaway: the chain builds a pile of protons, and ATP synthase makes ATP as they flow back. Protons flow back through ATP synthase only as fast as the cell spends ATP, and the chain slows when the pile is high. So the pile links how fast your cells use oxygen to how fast they spend ATP. That is why your cells use more oxygen when you work harder.

This model is simplified. A real chain has more parts than the three drawn here: four large protein complexes plus small carriers that shuttle electrons between them. The energy from each NADH pumps about ten protons, and ATP synthase uses a few protons for each ATP.
